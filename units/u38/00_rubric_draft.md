# Unit 38 — Stage 0 Draft Rubric

**Unit:** Mathematics Stage 3 Year B — Unit 38
**Topic:** **Chance & Data** — collecting and tabulating data; assigning probabilities to outcomes (including
not-equally-likely) using fractions and benchmark FDP; frequency vs probability; creating random generators;
comparing observed and expected frequencies and the role of randomness over small vs large numbers of trials;
fairness of chance games; sampling with replacement; interpreting and comparing data displays (side-by-side
column graphs, range, mode, shape of distribution); and identifying bias and misleading data in the media.
**Source:** `DoE Unit 38.zip` → `Mathematics-Stage3-Unit38.docx`, 8 lessons (per-lesson "Syllabus — Learning
intentions and success criteria" tables carrying the sub-strand label + A/B group).
**Strands / outcomes (from the SC tables + the "Outcomes and content" grid, Table 22):**
- `MA3-CHAN-01` — *conducts chance experiments with both small and large numbers of trials, records and compares observed and expected results, and assigns probabilities using fractions* — content group **B** (all chance content here)
- `MA3-DATA-01` — *collects and organises data, and creates and interprets tables and graphs* — content group **A**
- `MA3-DATA-02` — *interprets data displays, including timelines and line graphs* — content group **B**
- `MAO-WM-01` — Working mathematically (throughout)

> **A/B read from the DoE "Outcomes and content" grid (Table 22), not from difficulty.** The grid puts
> **Data A** (collect categorical/discrete numerical data; tabulate data) in **group A**; and **Data B**
> (interpret side-by-side column graphs; compare displays by range/mode/shape; interpret/identify bias and
> misleading data in the media) and **all Chance** content in **group B**.
>
> **Banding (policy set 2026-06-20).** Every **group A** content point goes in **Red** (mandatory) — so R1–R2
> are the Data A content. Group B is then banded by **SOLO cognitive demand**: the foundational, single-step
> chance work (assigning a probability as a fraction; the term *frequency* and frequency-vs-probability) sits
> in **Red** even though it is group B (simple B in Red is allowed); the applying/reasoning group-B work
> (designing random generators, observed-vs-expected experiments, fairness, sampling, interpreting/comparing
> displays, spotting bias) sits in **Yellow**. Green = Stage 4 **Core** content.

## 0a. Flat pool of extracted success criteria ("Students can …")

Extracted verbatim from each lesson's "Syllabus — Learning intentions and success criteria" table,
de-duplicated, with the sub-strand + A/B group noted.

1. record what could happen in chance experiments where some outcomes are more likely than others, and show the probability of each outcome using fractions *(Chance-B; L1)*
2. use knowledge of benchmark fractions, decimals and percentages to show how likely an outcome is *(Chance-B; L1)*
3. collect data through observations *(Data-A; L2)*
4. create random generators to follow specified probabilities or proportions *(Chance-B; L2)*
5. compare the frequency and the probability of an outcome in a chance experiment *(Chance-B; L3)*
6. explain why the results of a chance experiment might be different from what you expect, and how this relates to randomness *(Chance-B; L3)*
7. determine and discuss the differences between the expected probabilities and the observed probabilities after both small and large numbers of trials *(Chance-B; L3, L4)*
8. use the term frequency to describe the number of times a particular outcome occurs in a chance experiment *(Chance-B; L4)*
9. discuss the fairness of simple games involving chance and the idea of randomness *(Chance-B; L4)*
10. determine the possible make-up of a large collection by taking samples and putting them back each time before sampling again (sampling with replacement) *(Chance-B; L5)*
11. record collected data without using digital technologies (tabulate data) *(Data-A; L5)*
12. analyse and explain data displays found in digital media and factual texts *(Data-B; L6)*
13. identify sources of possible bias *(Data-B; L6)*
14. recognise data displays that may be misleading or biased *(Data-B; L6)*
15. interpret side-by-side column graphs with 2 different values *(Data-B; L7)*
16. interpret data representations found in digital media *(Data-B; L7)*
17. identify the shape of the distribution, including the range and the most frequent value (mode) in different data displays *(Data-B; L8)*

## 0b–0c. Classified, banded rubric

Format per outcome: **Code — Title** · descriptor · band · **[modality tag]** ·
*(sub-strand + content group + content point cited)*

### 🔴 RED — group A data (mandatory Red) + foundational single-step chance

**R1 — Collect data by observation and survey**
*"I can collect categorical and numerical data by observing or by running a survey."*
Band: **Red** · **[hands-on-preferred]** *(conduct a class survey / tally)*
Covers SC 3. *Data **A** (`MA3-DATA-01`). Content point: "Collect ordinal or nominal categorical data, and discrete numerical data through observation or by conducting surveys."*

**R2 — Organise data in a table**
*"I can record and organise collected data in a table (a tally or frequency table), with or without a spreadsheet."*
Band: **Red** · **[written/digital-appropriate]** *(tally / frequency table)*
Covers SC 11. *Data **A** (`MA3-DATA-01`). Content point: "Tabulate collected data with and without the use of digital technologies such as spreadsheets."*

**R3 — Probability as a fraction**
*"I can write the probability of an outcome as a fraction (even when outcomes are not equally likely), and use benchmark fractions, decimals and percentages to say how likely it is."*
Band: **Red** *(group B, but foundational/single-step → Red)* · **[hands-on-preferred]** *(spinners, bags of counters)*
Covers SC 1, 2. *Chance **B** (`MA3-CHAN-01`). Content points: "Record the outcomes for chance experiments where the outcomes are not equally likely to occur and assign probabilities to the outcomes using fractions (denominators of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10)"; "Use knowledge of benchmark fractions, decimals and percentages to assign probabilities to the likelihood of outcomes."*

**R4 — Frequency and probability**
*"I can use the word frequency for how many times an outcome happens, and tell the difference between the frequency and the probability of an outcome."*
Band: **Red** *(group B, but foundational → Red)* · **[written/digital-appropriate]**
Covers SC 5, 8. *Chance **B** (`MA3-CHAN-01`). Content points: "Use the term frequency to describe the number of times a particular outcome occurs in a chance experiment"; "Distinguish between the frequency of an outcome and the probability of an outcome in a chance experiment."*

### 🟡 YELLOW — applying / reasoning (group B chance & data)

**Y1 — Create random generators**
*"I can design a random generator (spinner, bag of counters, dice) to follow a set of probabilities or proportions."*
Band: **Yellow** · **[hands-on-preferred]** *(design spinners / bags)*
Covers SC 4. *Chance **B** (`MA3-CHAN-01`). Content point: "Create random generators to follow specified probabilities or proportions."*

**Y2 — Observed vs expected results**
*"I can compare what actually happens (observed) with what I expect (expected), and explain why they differ — especially with small vs large numbers of trials."*
Band: **Yellow** · **[hands-on-required]** *(roll/spin many trials and tally)*
Covers SC 6, 7. *Chance **B** (`MA3-CHAN-01`). Content points: "Explain why observed frequencies of outcomes in chance experiments may differ from expected frequencies, and how this relates to randomness"; "Determine and discuss the differences between the expected probabilities and the observed probabilities after both small and large numbers of trials."*

**Y3 — Fairness and randomness in chance games**
*"I can discuss whether a simple chance game is fair, using the idea of randomness."*
Band: **Yellow** · **[hands-on-preferred]** *(play and judge chance games)*
Covers SC 9. *Chance **B** (`MA3-CHAN-01`). Content point: "Discuss the fairness of simple games involving chance and the idea of randomness."*

**Y4 — Sampling with replacement**
*"I can estimate the make-up of a large hidden collection by taking samples and replacing them each time."*
Band: **Yellow** · **[hands-on-required]** *(sample from a bag, replace, tally)*
Covers SC 10. *Chance **B** (`MA3-CHAN-01`). Content point: "Determine the likely make-up of a large collection of objects, by sampling objects and returning them to the collection before the next sample (sampling with replacement)."*

**Y5 — Interpret and compare data displays**
*"I can read side-by-side column graphs and compare data displays using the range, the mode and the shape of the distribution."*
Band: **Yellow** · **[written/digital-appropriate]**
Covers SC 15, 17. *Data **B** (`MA3-DATA-02`). Content points: "Interpret side-by-side column graphs for 2 categorical variables"; "Interpret and compare different displays in terms of the shape of the distribution, including the range and the most frequent value (mode)."*

**Y6 — Bias and misleading data in the media**
*"I can interpret data displays from the media and factual texts, and spot when a display is biased or misleading."*
Band: **Yellow** · **[written/digital-appropriate]**
Covers SC 12, 13, 14, 16. *Data **B** (`MA3-DATA-02`). Content points: "Interpret data representations found in digital media and in factual texts"; "Identify sources of possible bias in representations of data in the media"; "Identify misleading representations of data in the media."*

### 🟢 GREEN — Stage 4 Core (verified against NESA K–10 2022)

No DoE Unit 38 lesson teaches these — expected for Green. Green extends the chance work into Stage 4
**Probability** (`MA4-PRO-C-01`) and the data work into Stage 4 **Data classification and visualisation**
(`MA4-DAT-C-01`).

**G1 — Sample space and theoretical probability**
*"I can list the sample space for a chance experiment and write P(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes."*
Band: **Green** · **[written/digital-appropriate]**
Extends R3. *Stage 4 — **Probability** (`MA4-PRO-C-01`). Content points: "List the sample space for chance experiments"; "Express the probability of an event… as P(event) = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes"; "Recognise that probabilities range from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain)."*

**G2 — Observed (relative frequency) and complementary events**
*"I can find an observed probability as a relative frequency from repeated trials, and use complementary events: P(not A) = 1 − P(A)."*
Band: **Green** · **[written/digital-appropriate]**
Extends Y2. *Stage 4 — **Probability** (`MA4-PRO-C-01`). Content points: "Explain that observed probability is the relative frequency resulting from repeated trials… and determine observed probabilities"; "Verify that the sum of the probability of an event and its complement is a total of 1"; "Solve problems involving the probability of complementary events."*

**G3 — Classify data and choose the right graph**
*"I can classify a variable as numerical (discrete/continuous) or categorical (nominal/ordinal) and choose a graph that suits the data."*
Band: **Green** · **[written/digital-appropriate]**
Extends R1, R2, Y5. *Stage 4 — **Data classification and visualisation** (`MA4-DAT-C-01`). Content points: "Classify and describe variables as numerical or categorical… discrete or continuous… nominal or ordinal"; "Select the type of graph best suited to represent various single datasets and justify the choice of graph."*
